Privacy notice

Guest reading does not require an account. Pakperk stores only the data needed for the features a reader chooses.

Data used by the service

  • Accounts: the identity provider retains verified email for optional registration, login, and recovery; Pakperk stores identity-provider issuer and subject, a local user ID, public handle or display name, status, policy acceptance, and operational timestamps in its application database.
  • Library and To Read: account-to-paper relationships, five-state reading status, private save notes, lists, tags, reminders, revisions, removal tombstones, and retry-safe operation records.
  • Discovery and alerts: optional explicit interests, feedback-derived preferences, short-lived recommendation activity, explicitly saved queries, subscriptions, reading briefs, and in-app notifications.
  • Deep Reader and research memory: shared normalized/source-linked paper artifacts plus optional private annotations and notes, retained edit conflicts, re-anchor history, evidence cards, position/mode checkpoints, memory prompts/answers and review state, assistant conversations, and owner-bound provenance.
  • Comments: public normalized text, author, paper, status, versions, and timestamps.
  • Safety: reports, blocks, shared rate-limit records, moderation decisions, deletion state, and a redacted audit trail.
  • Operations: request IDs and content-free reliability and security telemetry.

Data Pakperk deliberately avoids

The Pakperk application profile database does not copy the identity-provider email. Pakperk does not collect address-book contacts, precise location, advertising ID, or an avatar. Personalization is off until chosen and can be reset. Access and refresh tokens are excluded from general preferences, SQLite, and Pakperk-managed telemetry.

Comment/report text, paper full text, model prompts, assistant questions/answers, source quotes, annotation/conflict/evidence/memory bodies, raw search/import text, public handles, and identity subjects are excluded from telemetry.

Production traffic uses TLS. Pakperk's mobile Drift database is not encrypted at the application layer: private research bodies are ordinary SQLite text protected by the OS app sandbox, device access control, platform file protection, and backup disablement/exclusion. SQLCipher is deferred pending a reviewed threat model and key/migration/recovery design. Authentication refresh/session material remains separate in platform secure storage.

Pakperk sends its telemetry provider only a sanitized error category, never a raw exception or stack. Uncaught fatal errors are not swallowed and may create an OS-managed Apple or Google crash diagnostic under platform settings and policy; that path is separate from Pakperk's custom telemetry.

For shared abuse limits on comments and expensive public paper actions such as preparation and chat, the application accepts a forwarded request-origin chain only from configured ingress proxies, evaluates it right-to-left, and otherwise falls back to the direct peer. It immediately keys the selected address without logging or persisting the raw value, then keeps only that pseudonym through the configured rate-limit window, at most 30 days. Production edge security/access logs may separately retain a source network address for up to 30 days. These identifiers are not used for advertising or tracking and are separate from identifier-free mobile telemetry.

Visibility and processors

Comments are public. Blocks are private to the blocking account. Reports and moderation records are restricted to authorized operators. Configured providers process data only for identity, database hosting, service delivery, security, or content-free telemetry.

Retention and deletion

  • Keyed request-origin abuse-limit scopes expire with their configured window, at most 30 days, and are removed by bounded maintenance.
  • Recoverable database, identity, and PITR backups expire within 35 days.
  • Anonymized security and moderation audit records expire after 90 days.
  • Content-free operational telemetry and platform/application logs expire after 30 days.
  • Assistant threads start with a 30-day expiry, can refresh only within 90 days of creation, and retain at most 50 messages.
  • Annotations/conflicts/re-anchor history, evidence cards, checkpoints, and memory items remain until individual deletion where supported or account deletion. Item tombstones clear private bodies; no shorter routine purge is claimed.
  • A separately backed-up signed deletion-authority record is kept for at least 400 days, and longer only until evidence proves no recoverable backup can recreate the account.

Deleting an account disables access immediately, revokes provider sessions, deletes the provider identity and its verified email, and removes account-owned Library/discovery data, private research artifacts and assistant history/provenance, public comments, safety state, and pending operations. Shared paper metadata, normalized documents, and shared derived artifacts may remain. The deletion authority contains a keyed identity fingerprint and encrypted provider recovery coordinates—not profile, research, or comment content—so the worker can purge a skewed provider/database restore.

When the relevant default-off Plan 03 account features are enabled, the account-authenticated research route provides a bounded JSON or Markdown export, with a per-paper manifest for oversized accounts. It includes private research artifacts, owner-bound assistant history/provenance, Library metadata/private save notes, and citation/original-source metadata; it excludes principal identifiers and reconstructed full papers. Plan 03 remains unavailable for production until the protected privacy/legal, live deletion and restore, and signed-device gates pass.

After every recoverable backup has aged out and deletion reapplication is verified, an operator-controlled final purge removes that authority and its encrypted coordinates. A restored backup is never returned to service until the current external authority is reapplied and deletion verification passes.

Your request

Pakperk is the service controller unless this deployment's monitored support contact identifies another operating entity. Use account deletion for removal or that contact for access, correction, privacy, or legal questions. Include an operation ID where possible; never send a password or token.
